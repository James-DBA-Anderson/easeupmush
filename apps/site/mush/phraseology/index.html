Pompey phraseology

Street NPCs (and some trainers) speak Portsmouth. Talking to one again cycles their extra lines before looping back to the opener, so nobody repeats themselves straight away. Keep lines short. Don't dump every slang word into every line — sprinkle it so it still reads.

The words

Phrase Meaning / use
Pompey Portsmouth. Locals say this; outsiders may still say Portsmouth / portsmuth.
mush A man / bloke. Caring (“Alright mush.”), threatening (“Oi mush.”), or having a laugh. Default friendly address on the street.
dinlo Idiot. Use rarely.
dense Thick / not intelligent. A dense lad talks slow, boasts, and misses the point (Steve). Direction for voice, not a word they say about themselves.
squinny Someone who complains about an “injustice” that's trivial to everyone else. You can be a squinny or squinny about something.
ease up mush Calm down. Good after a fight or when someone's winding up.
copped Lost temper. Can stretch to copped the needle. “He's copped.” “She's copped the needle.” Ongoing: copping. “Alright you don't need to cop about it.” “I'm not copping, you're copping.”
bang out Knock out with a punch (“I'd bang him out if he said that to me.”).
bang out of order Really not fair (“That's bang out of order.”). Different from bang out.
decked Knocked out. “He proper decked him mush.” Same fight slot as bang out; mix them.
batter Beat up (“I will batter you.”). Trainer challenge energy.
geezer A man. Can shorten to geez (“Oi geez.”). Same slot as mush; mix them.
lakes Good / crazy. Only people who were young in the 80s — obscure now; younger NPCs should not know it.
cushty Something's good. Rough speakers only (drunks, denser lads).
beard You don't believe someone. Can stretch to beard on tash (“Beard. Beard on tash.”).
chore Steal. “You going down town on the chore?” = are you going into town to nick stuff.
shant Get on the booze. “I love a shant.” “He can ever have a shant.” “I got shanted last night.” “You were shanted mush.”
shant on Cheers — let's go and drink. Said once the drinking's been settled on (“Shant on! Cheers mush.”). Also does as a shrug when something goes wrong and there's always the pub: “Oh well. Shant on.”
chuffed Happy / proud (“I'm well chuffed with that.”). If they're too pleased: “Alright mush, what do you want, a chuffdy badge?”
dosh Money (“Aint got the dosh.”).
init Isn't it. Tag on the end of a line (“Cushty init.”).
dane tane Down town. How it sounds when spoken (“I've been dane tane on the shant mush.”). Pairs with chore / shant.

th → f

Drunks and thicker lads replace th with f in speech — fink, nuffin, wiv, portsmuth. Not every street voice: nans, Sir, Choke and Mum stay clearer. Examples: “I aint never been to portsmuth before.” “Who's that mush fink he is?”

Who uses what

Steve (2nd Avenue) is a dense lad — cushty, init, beard, fings. Street lads and trainers lean mush / geezer plus fight talk (decked, copped). Kay, the give-way bloke and the bus lad squinny; Tom's challenge takes a rare dinlo; Gaz and Mick bang out; Lee and Kay bang out of order; Gaz and Ryan batter; Dave, Mick and Ryan take losses with ease up mush; Dave and Mick shant and cushty, and Dave takes a win or a hiding with shant on; older nans and 80s voices get a rare lakes; denser lads get beard, chore, dane tane, init and chuffed.
